# Case study: $57.7M vs $9.0M on the same roster

**Question:** 76ers carry both Jaylen Brown ($57.7M) and Dean Wade ($9.0M) into 2026-27. Using the dashboard, how much on-court impact does each dollar buy per player?

All figures: 2025-26 regular season (Player Metrics page), 2026-27 salaries (Salary Cap page, PHI).

## The numbers

| | Jaylen Brown | Dean Wade |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-27 salary | **$57.7M** | **$9.0M** |
| 2025-26 team | BOS | CLE |
| Games / minutes | 71 GP · 34.4 MPG | 59 GP · 22.3 MPG |
| Box score | 28.7 PTS · 6.9 REB · 5.1 AST | 5.8 PTS · 4.2 REB · 1.5 AST |
| True shooting | 57.3% | 58.0% |
| **RAPM total** (pts/100 poss.) | **−0.0** | **+3.2** |
| Offense / defense RAPM | +1.7 / −1.8 | +1.0 / +2.2 |
| RAPM, no box-score prior | +0.2 | +3.1 |
| Tightly guarded % | 37.6% | 12.7% |
| Teammate rim shots / eFG when on court | +4.0 / +2.8 | −1.1 / +1.8 |

## What it says

1. **Wade's impact is real, not a box-score artifact.** His RAPM is +3.2 with the box-score prior and +3.1 without it. His stat line is modest; the lift comes from what happens around him, mostly on defense (+2.2).
2. **Brown's production doesn't show up in the scoreboard math.** 28.7 ppg nets out to zero RAPM: his offense (+1.7) is cancelled out by his defense (−1.8).
3. **Per dollar, their gap is wide.** Wade costs 6.4× less and posts +3.2 more points per 100 possessions. That's almost **+0.36 RAPM per $1M** for Wade versus **~0** for Brown.
4. **Brown has value that RAPM undervalues.** Defenses guard him tightly 37.6% of the time (Wade: 12.7%), and teammates get +4.0 more rim shots with him on the floor. That court gravity is why he's dangerous on the field and why RAPM alone shouldn't set his price.

## Caveats

- **RAPM ranges are wide.** Wade's 90% model range sits above zero; Brown's does not. Neither number is exact, but they differ in sign confidence.
- **Different roles and contexts.** Wade played 12 fewer minutes per game, alongside an elite CLE defense. Brown carried a high-usage role in BOS as a first-option scorer.
- **Replaceability matters.** A +3 role player is easier to find than a 28-point creator; salary also prices scarcity, not just impact.
- **Box-score defense is weak** (see Methodology), which is why Wade's +2.2 defensive RAPM leans on the on/off data.

## Takeaway for a front office

PHI is hard-capped at the first apron and $0.5M over it. In that position, the cheapest impact on the books is Wade's. Brown's contract is a bet on shot-creation and floor gravity that don't translate onto impact numbers yet.
